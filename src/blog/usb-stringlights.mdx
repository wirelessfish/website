Are we seriously using disposable batteries in the 26? Unreal. Not only does this create unnecessary waste, but it doesn't even make any sense! Rechargeable batteries
are dirt cheap now. Some manufacturers even make AA sized li-ion batteries with a USB-C port integrated into the body! Why are we not using this?!

Anyways I've gotten these dirt cheap string lights (5m) for about €2.5 (christmas is coming up, gotta get some decor going).
I've accepted that they require AA batteries, otherwise they wouldn't be that cheap. However once the batteries ran out after roughly a week
of use, I had a thought. Why doesn't this use a rechargeable battery?

So I got to work. I didn't have a charging module on hand, so I threw in a USB-C module to directly power the lights. They don't need to move much and are always
within reach of a power outlet anyway. The three batteries used to be connected in series, producing about 4.8V at most (AA batteries are labeled 1.5V, but fresh out
the box they can produce up to about 1.6V). The USB producing 5V is not that big of a problem, the chip on the PCB is probably rated for 5V anyway. But it made the
ligts draw more current than on the batteries, which _could_ lead to a shorter lifespan of the LEDs (more heat produced). So I added a 20 Ohm resistor to
somewhat limit the current (from 77mA to 55mA not much but better than nothing + the percieved brightness is pretty much the same).

phone charger!

[Here's](/assets/post/usb-stringlights/stringlights_bottom.3mf) the model for the bottom part and [here's](/assets/post/usb-stringlights/stringlights_top.3mf)

Also don't forget that heatshrink shrinks when exposed to heat. So don't be a smartass like me, putting it on the wire you're soldering just for it to tighten in the middle
of the wire instead of where you want it.
